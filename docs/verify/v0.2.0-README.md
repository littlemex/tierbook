of evidence: whether each entry's defect is gone, and whether the mechanism works against a real engine.

## Half two: the engine

`v0.2.0-engine-verify.json` and `v0.2.0-observe-readings.json`.

**`observe` reads occupancy, and it is a reading rather than a constant.** Over 400 samples against the loaded
engine, `inflight` took **19 distinct values from 2 to 20**. One reading could not have shown this: a constant is
indistinguishable from a measurement until it moves, so the run records the distinct values rather than a mean.

**A variable it could not read is absent with a reason.** Asked for a model the engine is not serving,
`inflight` and `arrival_rate_per_hour` come back in `not_observed`, each naming the metric and the label set that
was absent, and the observation reports `complete: false`. Nothing is defaulted to zero — a fabricated zero for
`inflight` reads as an idle engine and sends the next request to the reserved candidate.

**The documented path runs in order.** `validate`, `compile`, `observe`, and `observe --previous` for a rate. The
first `observe` refuses the arrival rate and says why; the second, given the first, produces one.

**`accept` records what it checked against.** Floor `0.8` and evidence-age limit `90`, both with a provenance line
naming the artifact they were read from, and `ignored_keys` empty because this reader understood every field it saw.

**The label is not task success and the artifact says so.** It records whether the engine returned a non-empty
completion, which is the observable the escalation rule already names. Judging the answer needs an oracle this
project does not ship, and attaching a success label anyway would put a number nobody measured at the centre of a
verification report.

## What was NOT verified, and why

An honest gap is information. A gap presented as coverage is the failure this phase exists to prevent.

**Exploration's live behaviour is not verified.** Over 400 live decisions the draw returned `no_eligible_arm` every
time and the diverted share was 0, against a declared rate of 0.05. That is correct behaviour and it is not evidence
about the draw. The cause is recorded because it is a real limitation nobody had written down:

`serve.candidate_set` builds the set from **the policy's own rules and default**, and the policy compiled from the
shipped ledger has **no rules** — the compiler refused to certify anything for that family on a 20-item cohort. So
the set has exactly one member, the eligible set has no alternative, and the draw correctly declines. **Exploration
can only reach a candidate the policy already names.** Whether that is right is a design question rather than a
defect: routing paid traffic into a candidate a held-out fold refused is what the compiler's refusal exists to
prevent. But it means the shipped example cannot exercise the draw, and it means the one-way door A3 describes is
open only for a candidate that still has a rule.

The draw's arithmetic *is* verified, hermetically, over 5,000 seeds at rate 0.05: the reason `explored` appears on
5.52% of draws and diversion is 5.52%, against a declared 5%. That is a check on the mechanism and not on the seed
sequence, and it is what caught C10.

**`default_is_not_a_hiding_place` reports FAIL on this run's log**, at a 100% uncertified share against a 10%
tolerance. This is the criterion telling the truth about the fixture: the compiled policy is `certified: false` with
no rules, so every assignment goes to the default. It is reported here rather than tuned away.

**C13 is not fixed and the release says so.** A caller can pass a bound the evidence does not support and
`no_false_certification` will pass it, because the falsifier computes admissibility from the caller's own number.
Amendment 16 records why it is deferred rather than absorbed: the artifact does not carry the absolute bound SCOPE
section 2 clause 1 names, only a paired difference against a reference, so closing it changes what a compiled policy
claims. That is a design change and it goes to the next release's phase 1.
