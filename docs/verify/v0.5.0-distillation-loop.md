# The distillation loop: Prismyra teaches, a LightGBM student serves, tierbook decides when

**The claim under test.** On a routine, closed-label task type, Prismyra can label traffic in the background, its
LightGBM student can be refitted on that log as it grows, and tierbook can certify, window by window, which arm is the
cheapest per accepted task at a declared floor — and say so when the student is not good enough yet.

Every round was pre-registered before it ran; the pre-registrations are summarised here with each round. All runs on
2026-11-09.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| task | `zeroshot/twitter-financial-news-sentiment` (MIT): bearish / bullish / neutral |
| traffic stream | the train split, shuffled (seed 1), nine windows of 1,000 items |
| certification set | 1,200 validation items, never in the stream; tierbook splits it into selection and held-out halves |
| admission eval set | 300 further validation items, used by Prismyra's admission and for choosing a recipe; never by tierbook |
| teacher | Prismyra `littlemex/prismyra-decision-35b-a3b-fp8` on one L40S |
| other arms | `qwen3-next-80b`, `claude-haiku-4-5` (metered, through a gateway); `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-FP8` on vLLM (self-hosted) |
| prices | Prismyra and vLLM: their share of the GPU node's on-demand price over measured items per hour; students: a c5a.large's price over measured CPU items per hour; APIs: the gateway's rates |
| declarations | floor 0.85 (rounds 1-3), alpha 0.05, Wilson, holdout 0.5, `unobserved_as: rejected` |

## Round 1: Prismyra's default student

The student plateaued at about 0.71 gold accuracy (Prismyra 0.85) from 1,000 to 9,000 items, and no window's student
passed Prismyra's own admission bar (0.90 / 0.90). The confidence-routed arm could not run: Prismyra refuses any
non-admitted student, including in `confidence` mode. tierbook certified **Prismyra** in every window at
**$0.0000469 per accepted task**; neither API arm (68.9 % and 75.8 % on the certification set) nor the generation model
cleared the floor.

## Round 3: how the student is raised

One factor at a time from round 1's recipe, every fit through `prismyra.learn.fit.fit_student`, scored on the admission
eval set (300 items), never on the certification set. At window 9:

| recipe | student accuracy | student + Prismyra at tau 0.8 | student's share at tau 0.8 | agreement with Prismyra, lower bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hashed TF-IDF, 256 dims (round 1's) | 0.733 | 0.823 | 0.46 | 0.743 |
| hashed, 4,096 dims | 0.770 | 0.830 | 0.57 | 0.806 |
| `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` embeddings | 0.803 | 0.837 | 0.69 | 0.843 |
| `google/embeddinggemma-2` embeddings | 0.807 | 0.837 | 0.74 | 0.835 |
| embeddinggemma-2, hard teacher labels | **0.830** | 0.837 | **0.85** | 0.837 |
| 600 rounds / hard or margin-weighted labels / 5 % gold feedback / teacher on half the items (each on the hashed features) | 0.71-0.75 | 0.82-0.83 | 0.44-0.71 | 0.74-0.75 |

The features carry almost all of it: sentence embeddings, registered through Prismyra's
`prismyra.learn.features.register_features`, take the student from 0.73 to 0.81-0.83. Hard teacher labels on those
features make the student confident on more items (85 % at tau 0.8 against 74 %) at the same accuracy. The other
factors did not move the hashed student. The recipe chosen by the pre-registered rule (eval accuracy) was
embeddinggemma-2 with hard labels.

tierbook on the certification set, per window, floor 0.85:

| windows | certified arm | spend per accepted task |
|---|---|---|
| 1-7 | Prismyra | $0.0000469 |
| 8-9 | student + Prismyra (confidence), **unconfirmed**: selection cleared the floor, the held-out lower bound (0.829-0.832) did not | $0.0000112 |

The student alone reached 0.80 on the certification set. The confidence-routed arm, which lets the student answer the
80 % it is sure of and Prismyra the rest, reached 0.86 at about a quarter of Prismyra's cost; 600 held-out items are not
enough to show 0.86 is above 0.85, and tierbook said so rather than certifying it.

## Round 4, idea A: teach on a GPU for a while, then serve the student

The teacher's GPU bill becomes a one-off: labelling 9,000 items took $0.37 at the measured Prismyra throughput, added to
the student's per-attempt price amortised over a declared horizon of 1,000,000 requests. Accepting accuracy loss is a
declared floor, so tierbook was run at three:

| floor | certified at window 9 | spend per accepted task |
|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | student + Prismyra, unconfirmed (as above) | $0.0000113 |
| 0.80 | student + Prismyra, unconfirmed (held-out lower bound 0.788) | $0.0000081 |
| 0.75 | **the CPU student alone (bge-small, hard labels), certified from window 3** | **$0.00000069**, about 1/68 of Prismyra |

The same evidence gives Prismyra at 0.85 and a GPU-free student at 0.75; the floor is the operator's to declare.

## Cost of the student's features

Single-item CPU embedding time with two threads (an x86 host): `google/embeddinggemma-2` 62.7 ms,
`BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` 15.6 ms; LightGBM's own prediction is under a millisecond. The embedding, not the booster, is
the student's cost, and the embeddinggemma-2 student is slower per item than Prismyra on a GPU (about 32 ms) while still
cheaper per item.

## Round 5: fine-tune a 0.8B LLM on Prismyra's labels, serve it on a CPU

Read out without training, `Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B` labels 55 % of 200 certification items correctly. Round 5 trains it
instead: full fine-tuning on one L40S, per window, on round 3's training pool with Prismyra's top label as the target
(gold labels never used), cross-entropy over the first token of each label, fixed hyperparameters (AdamW, lr 2e-5,
batch 32, two epochs, seed 0). Serving is one prefill on the CPU and an argmax over the three label tokens.

| window | items trained on | GPU seconds | admission eval accuracy | agreement with Prismyra, lower bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 900 | 89.0 (includes kernel compilation) | 0.830 | 0.863 |
| 3 | 2,700 | 55.2 | 0.827 | 0.857 |
| 9 | 8,100 | 157.7 | 0.843 | 0.911 |

On the full certification set (1,200 items) the window-9 model accepts **0.885**, against Prismyra's 0.896 and the
bge-small LightGBM student's 0.810. On a Graviton4 c8g.xlarge (PyTorch 2.15, `DNNL_DEFAULT_FPMATH_MODE=BF16`,
`LRU_CACHE_CAPACITY=1024`), one item takes 479.5 ms on 4 threads and 646.6 ms on 1 thread, and the CPU's label equals
the GPU's on all 200 items checked. Priced at $0.20012/h over 7,508 items/h plus the teaching cost amortised over
1,000,000 requests, an attempt costs $0.0000267, 0.64 of Prismyra's $0.0000416.

tierbook, per window, alongside every earlier arm:

| floor | outcome at window 9 | the 0.8B arms on the held-out half |
|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | the cheapest arm that cleared selection is the embedding student routed with Prismyra, **unconfirmed** | student alone 0.871 (lower bound 0.842); routed with Prismyra 0.877 (0.847): the floor is inside both intervals |
| 0.80 | the same routed embedding student, unconfirmed | the 0.8B student alone is **certified at window 1** at $0.0000319 per accepted task, then displaced by a cheaper arm that cleared selection |
| 0.75 | the bge-small student alone, certified from window 3, $0.00000069 | above the floor, but about 45 times the certified arm's cost |

The fine-tuned 0.8B is the closest a CPU arm came to its 35B teacher (1.1 points below it, against 8.6 for the
embedding student) at about two thirds of the teacher's cost, but nowhere is it the cheapest arm that is certified: at
0.85 its held-out lower bound is 0.003-0.008 short of the floor with 600 items, and at 0.80 and 0.75 an embedding student
an order of magnitude cheaper clears. tierbook checks the arm selection chose and does not try the next one on the same
held-out half, which is why window 2 onwards at 0.80 is unconfirmed rather than the 0.8B certified.

The window-9 checkpoint was not kept; the training script and its fixed seed are the record.

## Round 6: tune how the 0.8B is served, not what it answers

Round 5 priced the 0.8B from an untuned path, one item at a time. Round 6 changed only how it is served, on the same
Graviton4 c8g.xlarge (4 threads), and checked after every step that its labels on 200 certification items still equal
the GPU's. The window-9 model was re-trained with the same script and seed (round 5's checkpoint was not kept), and a
second model was trained with the headline alone as the prompt (37 tokens per item instead of 68).

| step, added to the previous | full prompt, items per hour | short prompt, items per hour | labels equal to the GPU's |
|---|---|---|---|
| round 5's path | 7,319 | 9,594 | 200 / 200 (short: 199) |
| logits of the three label tokens at the last position only | 9,233 | 11,984 | same |
| bf16 weights | 9,749 | 13,270 | same |
| Gated DeltaNet chunk as one triangular solve, chunk sized to the input | 12,261 | 17,264 | same |
| batches of 8 | **17,182** | **27,848** | same |

Where the time went: one item made about 47,000 small operator calls, and the Gated DeltaNet layers took 67 % of the
time. The reference chunk computation inverts a 64 x 64 triangular matrix one row at a time (63 Python iterations per
layer, 18 layers) and pads every input to a multiple of 64. Replacing the loop with one `solve_triangular` gives the
same matrix (relative error at most 5e-7 with the same chunking; up to 6e-3 on random inputs when the chunk length also changes,
which changed no label) and cut the calls to about 13,600. Batches of 32, four single-thread processes, a cached
instruction prefix, `torch.compile` and int8 dynamic quantisation were measured and were slower, equal, or changed
labels (int8 agreed with the GPU on 33-40 % of items); the cached prefix is the exception for one item at a time,
213 ms against 287 ms, which matters when requests arrive singly. After the change, dense matrix products are about half the time.

Priced at the fastest step that kept the labels, an attempt costs $0.0000116 (full prompt) and $0.0000072 (short
prompt), against Prismyra's $0.0000416. tierbook at floor 0.85 chose the short-prompt student alone for the first time
as the cheapest arm that cleared selection, and left it unconfirmed: held-out 0.854, lower bound 0.823.

## Round 7: the routing threshold is part of the arm

Prompted by Husain and Saha's
[How To Use Decision Models for Evals](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/decision-model-evals/) (a score's default threshold
is arbitrary and should be tuned on data the result is not reported on), the fixed tau 0.8 of every routed arm was
replaced by nine thresholds from 0.50 to 0.99, each its own candidate, built from the recorded probabilities with no new
inference. At 0.98-0.99 the routed 0.8B accepts 0.895 of the certification items, level with Prismyra's 0.896, with the
student answering 77-82 % of them.

None was certified. With 37 candidates clearing selection at floor 0.85, the cheapest was again the student alone, which
the held-out half did not confirm. More candidates made a lucky choice likelier, which is the selection effect the
article warns about and the held-out check exists to catch. The threshold has to be chosen on data the certificate is
not computed on, or declared from a few candidates.

## Round 8: cascades in tierbook

Round 7's eighteen routed arms were built by hand: a call file per threshold and a price per arm computed outside
tierbook from the whole-set share of items the teacher answered. tierbook now declares them as `cascade` candidates
(`primary`, `fallback`, `threshold`) and composes their attempts from the parts' own attempts, with the student's score
recorded on its calls. Replaying round 7 that way gave the same item and accepted counts as the hand-built arms for all
eighteen, on both halves, at each of the three floors (108 cells). Costs differ by up to 3 %, because a cascade prices
the items its teacher actually answered in each half rather than the whole-set share.

## Round 9: the threshold chosen on data the certificate never sees, one cascade per student

Each window-9 0.8B model (round 6's full-prompt model, re-trained, and the headline-only one) scored the 300-item
admission set, which is disjoint from the certification set. On those items every threshold from 0.50 to 0.99 gave the
cascade 0.84-0.85 against gold, so the set could not tell thresholds apart. The pre-registered rule (the cheapest
threshold whose Wilson lower bound on the 300 items clears the floor, else the one with the highest bound) chose 0.85
and 0.98 at floor 0.85, 0.85 and 0.60 at 0.80, and 0.50 for both at 0.75. Each was declared as one cascade and offered
with the arms a deployment at window 9 would have: Prismyra, the two 0.8B students alone, and the bge-small student
alone and routed.

| floor | tierbook's choice | the cascades on the held-out half |
|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | the headline-only 0.8B alone, cheapest to clear selection (lower bound 0.856 there), **unconfirmed** on held-out (0.823) | both clear the floor: 0.882 (lower bound 0.853) at $0.0000172 and 0.880 (0.851) at $0.0000175 per accepted task, against Prismyra's $0.0000469 |
| 0.80 | the headline-only 0.8B alone, **certified** at $0.0000090 per accepted task (held-out lower bound 0.823) | above the floor, but dearer |
| 0.75 | the bge-small student alone, certified at $0.00000099 | above the floor, about 9 times dearer |

At 0.80 a 0.8B LLM fine-tuned on Prismyra's labels is certified for the first time, at a fifth of Prismyra's cost per
accepted task. At 0.85 an arm that certifies at 37 % of Prismyra's cost exists and was not chosen: selection
takes the cheapest arm whose selection-half lower bound clears the floor, and only that one is checked on the held-out
half, so a cheap arm that barely clears selection hides a dearer one that would have held. With every earlier arm also
offered, the 0.85 and 0.80 choices were unconfirmed and 0.75 went to the bge-small student, as before.

## Round 10: a held-out ladder

Round 9's miss at 0.85 came from the selection rule, not the arms: only the cheapest arm that cleared selection was
checked. tierbook now takes a declared `holdout_ladder`: the k cheapest arms of the selection-half frontier, checked
on the held-out half dearest first at the full alpha, stopping at the first that does not clear (a fixed-sequence
test). On round 9's evidence, unchanged:

| floor | holdout_ladder 1 | holdout_ladder 3 or all |
|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | the headline-only 0.8B alone, unconfirmed | **the headline-only cascade (threshold 0.98), certified at $0.0000172 per accepted task**; the next rung, the full-prompt 0.8B alone, was not confirmed and stopped the ladder |
| 0.80 | the headline-only 0.8B alone, certified at $0.0000090 | the same: every rung confirmed down to it |
| 0.75 | the bge-small student, certified at $0.00000099 | the same |

At 0.85 the policy now serves a CPU student that answers 82 % of the certification items and hands the rest to Prismyra, at 37 % of
Prismyra's own cost per accepted task, with the same held-out guarantee.

## Round 2 and round 11: the real Prismyra router, certified and then served

Round 2 gave Prismyra a confidence-mode admission bar (the hybrid that a confidence router serves is judged, not the
student's full-coverage agreement) and a `--features-module` loader, on Prismyra's branch `feat/confidence-admission`.
With the same 0.90 / 0.90 bars, the bge-small embedding student was admitted in confidence mode at tau 0.8 from window
4 on. Prismyra's own server, running in confidence mode on that artifact, answered the 1,200 certification items for
real at windows 4, 5, 6 and 9, the student serving 63-84 % of them.

| window | real router's share answered by the student | held-out rate (lower bound) | at floor 0.85 | at floor 0.80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 65 % | 0.861 (0.831) | Prismyra certified | the offline routed student certified, cheaper |
| 5 | 84 % | 0.863 (0.833) | Prismyra certified | **the real router certified, $0.0000084** |
| 6 | 82 % | 0.865 (0.834) | Prismyra certified | **the real router certified, $0.0000094** |
| 9 | 63 % | 0.868 (0.838) | Prismyra certified (the router cleared selection and not held-out) | the offline routed student certified, cheaper |

At 0.85 round 2's success condition did not hold, so its export step did not run. Round 11 ran it at 0.80 on window
6: `tierbook export-policy --target prismyra-learn` wrote the confidence router into Prismyra's learn spec, Prismyra's
server started on it (115 s), and 300 routed items never seen in training or certification were answered, 203 by the
student (median 13.8 ms) and 97 by Prismyra (median 45.2 ms). `tierbook drift` against the deployed policy: 263 of 300
accepted, lower bound 0.835 against the floor 0.80, **confirmed**; no re-derivation change, no unattributed attempts.

## Round 12: a 0.8B cascade served by vLLM Semantic Router's confidence looper

The read-out students of rounds 5-10 answer by comparing three label logits, so no OpenAI-compatible router can serve
them: asked to generate, the round-6 model produced "1111", because its training never asked the label to win over
the rest of the vocabulary. Round 12 re-trained the headline-only model with the loss changed to next-token
cross-entropy over the full vocabulary on "<label><|im_end|>" (same data, seed and hyperparameters; 1,194 GPU seconds),
served it with vLLM 0.27.1, and gave Prismyra an OpenAI-compatible `/v1/chat/completions` (Prismyra branch
`feat/openai-chat-completions`). All 1,200 certification answers were one of the three labels, in a mean 32.8 ms.

The score is the router's own: vLLM Semantic Router's `avg_logprob` confidence, `clamp((mean token logprob + 3) / 3)`,
read in its source at the commit of the development build used (`37bdd81`), and recorded from the served endpoint with
the request the router forwards. Round 9's rule on the 300-item admission set chose threshold 0.98 at floor 0.85.

| step | result |
|---|---|
| certification, round 9's arms plus this student | not chosen: priced on its GPU deployment (a quarter of a g6e.12xlarge over 109,908 items/h) it costs $0.0000418 per accepted task, about Prismyra's; the cheaper certified arms are read-out students this router cannot serve |
| certification, the arms this router can serve (amendment written after the row above) | **the cascade certified at 0.85**: held-out lower bound 0.851, $0.0000465 per accepted task |
| `export-policy --target vsr --cascade-confidence avg_logprob --signal 'finnews-sentiment=*'` | one decision, `algorithm.type: confidence`, the student then Prismyra, threshold 0.98; the router's structural validation passed |
| router in the cluster (`vllm-sr serve --target kubernetes --platform cpu --minimal`), 300 routed items | all answered; the router chose the student for 269 and Prismyra for 31, and on **all 300** that choice equals "recorded score >= 0.98" for the same item asked directly; the 269 student answers equal the direct ones |
| `tierbook drift` | 267 of 300 accepted, lower bound 0.8495 against the floor 0.85: **consistent** (not contradicted, not yet confirmed) |

The first deployment attempt asked for a GPU (`--platform auto` chose CUDA), and Karpenter started a g6e.2xlarge for
it; it was removed empty after about six minutes.

## Round 13: the generating 0.8B on a CPU

Round 12's recipe was re-trained (its checkpoint was not kept); the new model's certification labels equal round 12's
on 93.4 % of items, training not being bit-reproducible. On a Graviton4 c8g.xlarge (PyTorch, bf16, the triangular-solve
Gated DeltaNet chunk, greedy decoding to `<|im_end|>`), its answers equal vLLM's on 200 of 200 items and the router's
decision (confidence >= 0.98) on 199 of 200, so the CPU path serves the same answers as the certificate was computed on.

| CPU path | items per hour | price per attempt | floor 0.85 (cascade, threshold 0.99) | floor 0.80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| batches of 8 (pre-registered) | 7,105 | $0.0000298 | cascade **unconfirmed**: held-out 0.878, lower bound 0.849 | the student alone **certified**, $0.0000348 per accepted task, 0.74 of Prismyra's |
| one at a time (measured after, not pre-registered) | 12,918 | $0.0000171 | the same, at $0.0000261 | the same, at $0.0000200, 0.43 of Prismyra's |

After the pre-registered measurement, a profile of one batch of 8 found 2.6 of its 3.9 seconds in three products of
`[8, 1024] x [1024, 248320]`: the output layer over the full vocabulary, at 871 ms each, against 52 ms each for 16
rows. oneDNN's bf16 path on this CPU picks a far slower kernel for 8 rows. Padding the output layer's rows to a
multiple of 16 (the padded rows dropped, the logits unchanged), and computing that layer for the last position only,
gives 19,612 items per hour with answers equal to vLLM's on 200 of 200 and the router's decision on 199 of 200. At
that price ($0.0000119 per attempt) the generating student alone is certified at 0.80 at $0.0000138 per accepted task,
0.30 of Prismyra's; at 0.85 the cascade stays unconfirmed (lower bound 0.849) at 0.43 of Prismyra's.

Generation costs a CPU student more than reading three label logits did (rounds 5-10 certified those at $0.0000090),
because each answer is two or three decode steps; with batches, padding to the longest prompt made it slower still.
The router can serve this student; it could not serve the cheaper read-out one.

## Round 14: a paid-for second look at 0.85

At 0.85 round 13's cascade (the generating 0.8B, threshold 0.99, then Prismyra) was unconfirmed at a held-out lower
bound of 0.849. The validation split's 588 items in no set so far joined the certification set (1,788 items, the same
salt, so every earlier item stayed in its half), and since this is a second look after seeing the first, every arm was
re-derived at alpha 0.025 (Bonferroni over the two looks), so the guarantee holds at 0.95 across both.

| floor | result on 887 held-out items, alpha 0.025 |
|---|---|
| 0.85 | **the cascade certified**: held-out 0.878, lower bound 0.8515, $0.0000191 per accepted task, 0.41 of Prismyra's ($0.0000467); the next rung, the student alone, was not confirmed (0.825) |
| 0.80 | the generating student alone certified, $0.0000139 per accepted task, 0.30 of Prismyra's |

The new Prismyra answers came from a re-created pod whose image no longer carried some packages the experiment's
environment relied on (scipy, scikit-learn's dependencies, `kernels`); with them installed, Prismyra re-answered 100
earlier certification items and agreed with round 1 on 97.

## Still running when this was written

Nothing. The read-out 0.8B cascades of rounds 5-10 remain certified on their CPU price but are not servable by an
OpenAI-compatible router; round 12's generating student is, at a GPU price, and its CPU price is not yet measured.
