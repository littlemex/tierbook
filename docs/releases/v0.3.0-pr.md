# v0.3.0 — the mechanism stops claiming more than it holds, and the candidate set comes from the ledger

| | |

## Why

The previous release shipped exploration and could not verify it. Over 400 live decisions the draw returned
`no_eligible_arm` every time and the diverted share was 0.0 against a declared 0.05, because `serve.candidate_set`
built its set from `policy.rules` and the policy compiled from the shipped ledger has no rules — the compiler
certifies nothing on a 20-item cohort. A candidate with no rule was absent from the record entirely: invisible to
exploration, never labelled, its evidence never refreshing. The mechanism that existed to gather data about arms it
had not chosen could not reach one.

That was the visible failure. Underneath it was a pattern the release's own phase-1 review kept finding in different
organs: **the mechanism recorded claims nothing checked.** A bound carried a free-text `bound_kind`, so three records
with the same fabricated bound of 0.99 and three different stories about where it came from all certified
identically. A record carried four version-shaped fields and could not name the artifact that produced it, so the
falsifier's own premise — "the record says which artifact decided this" — was unimplementable as written, and had
been written as though it held. A declared floor above `alpha ** (1/n)` can never be cleared whatever the evidence
says, and an impossible floor produced byte-identical output to no floor at all.

So this release is about the mechanism's honesty, in the order the dependencies force: one meaning for `certified`, a
record that can name its artifact, and a tenant term whose cardinality is declared, before anything that computes a
bound. Those three are prerequisites rather than companions, and the argument rests on the log being append-only —
a claim recorded once cannot be corrected later, so it has to be right when written.

## What changed

**A bound says what produced it, and `certified` stops meaning two things.** `bound_kind` was a free string and
nothing read it. `record.BoundProvenance` replaces it with an estimator, a significance and the corrections the
bound was actually corrected over, each drawn from a closed vocabulary, and `record.admissible` reads it: a
provenance naming a correction this mechanism never performed is refused as `unearned_correction` rather than merely
recorded. Both directions of every pairing are refused, so the unattributed state is not representable rather than
labelled — a bound with no provenance, a provenance with no bound, an `unrecorded` estimator claiming a correction,
and (found in the audit) a named estimator with no significance, because the same estimator at 0.05 and at 0.20 is
two different claims wearing one name.

`certified` is now SCOPE section 2's judgment and nothing else. It had been copied from `policy.validated`, the
compile-time non-inferiority status, on the deterministic path — which is the majority of decisions — so renaming
the field alone would have left two words with one silently carrying the other's meaning. It is computed as the
conjunction of the rules having cleared non-inferiority and the served candidate being admissible: admissibility is
a property of the candidate, validation is a property of the rules that reached it, and dropping either is the same
defect from a different side. Measured through the loop, a candidate whose bound is 0.10 against a floor of 0.80
used to come back `certified: true`.

A consequence worth stating: with a floor declared, **`route_once` can no longer produce a falsely-certified record
at all**, because the decision and the falsifier now share one predicate. The falsifier is still tested, against a
row reaching the log by another writer — `Decision` accepts that combination on purpose, because refusing it at
construction would move the check into the writer and leave nothing able to audit a log written by an older
mechanism version or a second implementation.

**Reading an old log is not certifying a new decision, and auditing an old record is neither.** Refusing the
unattributed bound at construction made every historical row unreadable; supplying a provenance on read then made
every historical certified decision fail the falsifier — not only the wrong ones — so a correct old log accused the
mechanism, which SCOPE section 12 reads as evidence the mechanism is broken. A row written before this vocabulary
existed reads with an `unrecorded` estimator, and `check_certification` separates "was not admissible" from "cannot
be verified": `accept.no_false_certification` returns `UNSUPPORTED` over a population it cannot check and never
`FAIL`, because a falsifier whose silence is read as evidence must not speak from absence. A v0.1.0 `bound_kind` is
reported as an unmodelled field and deliberately **not** translated into a provenance — turning an unchecked string
into a recorded estimator would launder the exact claim this entry exists to stop.

**A compiled artifact hashes itself, and the record carries that hash.** `decide.policy_digest` is a sha256 over the
artifact's own serialisation, truncated the way `policy.registry_version` already truncates; `compile_policy` stamps
it; `Decision.policy_digest` carries it, required at `schema_version` 3 and supplied as empty for a version 1 or 2
row. `route_once` derives it and refuses a caller-supplied `policy_version` that disagrees, and `--policy-version`
loses its `"unversioned"` default, because a value the mechanism can derive is not a value a caller supplies. The
refusal is two refusals and not one: an artifact that recorded nothing cannot confirm a supplied value, and an
artifact that recorded a different value contradicts it — collapsing them would report an absence as a rival claim.

**The tenant term's cardinality is declared.** SCOPE section 6's multiplicity family includes a tenant term, and a
family could say nothing about it. Each family now declares `tenant_scope` as `single`, `pooled` or `per_tenant`;
the omission is refused naming the field, and `per_tenant` combined with an `exploration_rate` is refused naming
both. Nothing acts on the declaration yet, and the interface says so: what this closes is the gap between an
assumption and a statement.

**The ceiling is computed, recorded, and read by a criterion.** `accept.floor_reachable(n, floor, alpha)` returns
whether the floor is reachable at all on a cohort of `n`, the ceiling `alpha ** (1/n)`, and the smallest cohort that
would reach it. `compile_policy` records all three. `accept.CRITERIA` gains `floor_is_reachable` as a tenth
criterion, which **reads** them — shipping the ceiling as another `"warning"` string was rejected, because neither
existing warning key is read by anything and a recorded-and-unread number is the defect `bound_kind` was.

It does not refuse at compile time. An unreachable floor is a true fact about the evidence budget rather than a
malformed declaration, and SCOPE sections 2, 8 and 12 all make serving the declared default uncertified the correct
behaviour in that state — section 12 makes it a *passing* criterion. Refusing would turn a purpose-correct
degenerate state into an outage.

SCOPE section 12 grows from nine criteria to ten and says why the tenth is computed from the compiler rather than
from the log. The previous release deliberately kept `floor_compliance` as one criterion carrying two rates to
avoid a tenth; that decision is reversed here for a different reason — two rates over one population are one
question asked twice, and floor reachability is a different question about a different object.

**An operator can produce a labelled log using only the documented surface.** `Log.attach_outcome` and
`record.classify_label` existed, a family declared a labeller, and there was no CLI verb and no mention in
`README.md`, `SCOPE.md` or any `--help` — so every criterion needing a realised rate was permanently `unsupported`
for anyone working from the documented surface, and the report attributed that to a missing measurement rather than
to a missing door. `tierbook attach-outcome` attaches an outcome. It does not read a family's `label_source`, does
not invoke anything, and does not decide `label_state`: running somebody's benchmark suite is a boundary this
project states in its own first paragraph.

Two refusals the door promises did not exist where the door needed them. Appending a label that contradicts a
recorded one was **accepted**, with the refusal arriving from a later `Log.read()` — by which point the log is
unreadable, every criterion over it fails, and append-only means the operator cannot take it back. An outcome
naming a request the log does not contain was accepted too, so the label landed on nothing. The verb reads the log
before it appends and refuses both. `Log.read()`'s own refusal stays and gains a report: the door catches this
operator's mistake with a message they can act on, and the reader is the backstop for every other writer — and that
backstop covered the changed label and not the orphan, so `read()` now reports outcomes that matched no decision.
Reported rather than refused, because a rewritten label makes every criterion a criterion over the rewrite while an
orphan makes a label silently absent.

**The candidate set comes from the ledger's outcomes.** `policy.candidates_for` names every candidate the ledger
records an outcome for; `compile_policy` writes them into the artifact; `serve.candidate_set` reads them.
`from_dict` refuses an artifact with no `candidates` key rather than falling back to the rules — unconditionally,
because a rules-less artifact is the shape the incident produced, not the shape to exempt.

The set carries ids and no bound, and that is a correction to this release's own interface. Implemented as written
it computed a per-candidate absolute lower bound, which is the fixed-sample single-test quantity SCOPE section 6
disqualifies in those words and the contract's own rejected-alternatives table turns down — with the reason
attached, which is what caught it. Two measurements settled it: at 20 of 20 the bound is 0.8609, exactly the
cohort's own ceiling, so at the top of the range the number describes how many items were run rather than the
candidate it is filed under; and for the retail family's declared floor of 0.92 no candidate clears, an impossible
comparison the artifact would have asserted. Nothing read it either, so it would have been a second
recorded-and-unread number beside `bound_kind`. Membership is the whole claim, and `no_bound` is still derived at
decision time from the caller's own bounds.

## What the review found in the release itself

Thirteen amendments are appended to `CONTRACT.md`, and several correct sentences I had written from a mechanism's
intent without running them against it. The pattern is worth naming because it is the same defect class the release
is about, one level out.

- **SCOPE section 2 listed four admissibility clauses while the code enforced a fifth this release added.** The
  paragraph immediately after clause 4 exists *because* this happened once before — the document listed three
  conditions, the code enforced four, and a worker implementing exploration read "the rest of admissibility" as
  everything except freshness. Clause 5 is written now, and the falsifier's row names the clause list rather than a
  count.
- **An amendment claimed two named tests would pass unchanged.** They asserted `PASS` where the amendment's whole
  point is `UNSUPPORTED`.
- **An amendment named one of `decide.parameter`'s two refusals** and the implementation followed it, collapsing an
  absence into a rival claim.
- **An amendment justified keeping a backstop by saying it covered both of the door's two mistakes.** It covered
  one.
- **An amendment claimed this release removes two `"warning"` strings.** It does not touch them, and it should not:
  their reader is a person, and "nothing reads it" is not a defect for a sentence whose audience is human.
- **`cli.py` said "deliberately four verbs" over a list of eight that omitted three**, and this release added a
  ninth line while `--help` showed twelve. The count is removed rather than corrected, and the list's agreement
  with `--help` is now a test, because a hand-kept list beside the thing it lists has drifted twice.

## How it was verified

Nine reproduce/verify pairs, each observing the cause the contract names rather than a downstream symptom, at the
commit before that entry's fix and again at `HEAD`. Two entries cannot be observed at the previous release at all,
because C1 is the rename that gave `Policy` its `validated` field and `policy_digest` did not exist.

Then a real engine, because C6's whole subject is what a live occupancy reading makes possible. On a `g5.xlarge`
created for the run and destroyed after it, under 20 concurrent completions, over 200 decisions at the exploration
rate the shipped config declares:

| | previous release | this release |
|---|---|---|
| draws with an alternative available | **0 / 400** | **200 / 200** |
| traffic actually diverted | 0.0% | **10 of 200 = 5.0%**, against a declared 5% |
| candidates reached | 1 | 3 |
| records naming their artifact | — | 200 / 200, all carrying the artifact's own hash |

`floor_is_reachable` returned `pass` from the recorded ceiling. The door moved `floor_compliance`'s served
population from nothing to 40 labelled decisions with a 95% lower bound of 92.78%, while the criterion's overall
verdict stayed `unsupported` because `certified` is 0 on every decision — the shipped policy is not validated, and
that is the new conjunction working rather than the door failing.

`docs/verify/v0.3.0-README.md` also records eight defects in the verification harness itself, because every one of
them made an observation look like a result: a pair whose sides agree because the observation never reached the
thing under test reads exactly like a pair whose sides agree because the defect was never there.

## What is still open

The absolute bound is owed to a later release and the reason is recorded rather than left implicit: the estimator
available without a dependency is fixed-sample and single-test, and shipping it would relocate the defect rather
than close it — a caller's honest guess becomes the compiler's miscalibrated assertion, which is worse because it
carries the compiler's authority.

Retirement is absent. Age stands where a change point belongs, and SCOPE section 2's fourth clause currently
describes the placeholder as the requirement. That the document disagrees with itself is now written down.
