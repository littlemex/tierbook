# v0.1.0 — one turn of the loop runs end to end

| | |
|---|---|
| What this adds | A state collector, a decision record, an acceptance checker, and the function that calls them in order |
| What it does not add | Anything that feeds back. Outcomes are attachable and nothing attaches them; exploration is off; no arc runs from the record to the policy |
| Verified | Against a running vLLM engine, not a fixture: observe, observe again for a rate, assign, attach an outcome, check |
| Tests | 1,021 passing, 3 skipped (the skipped read `/proc` and were verified on Linux instead) |
| Acceptance | 3 of SCOPE section 12's 9 criteria evaluated and passing, 6 `unsupported`, 0 failing |

## Why

SCOPE section 3 states the mechanism as `route(request, state) -> assign(candidate, evidence, certified)`. Until now
`decide()` was handed a state by whoever called it, nothing wrote a record, and section 12's acceptance criteria were
prose. The mechanism could be described and not run. This release is the smallest thing that makes it run.

## What changed

**`observe` — the state a policy decides from.** Occupancy and availability from a vLLM `/metrics` endpoint, plus the
family's authorisation and evidence age.

Nothing is defaulted. A variable that could not be read is absent with a reason, and `decide` reports it as
`uncollected_variable` and declines to certify. That is the design rather than an omission: a fabricated zero for
`inflight` reads as an idle engine and sends the next request to the reserved candidate, so the one value a collector
must not guess is the one a naive implementation defaults. `observe` exits non-zero on an incomplete state, so a deploy
script cannot proceed on a state nobody looked at.

Four refusals worth naming, each from a way the number would otherwise be plausible and wrong:

- `inflight` counts `running` **and** `waiting`. `waiting` above zero means the seats are full, and counting only
  `running` reports a saturated engine as having room.
- Summing across `model_name` is refused when several series exist and no filter was given, because the total would be
  another candidate's occupancy added to this one's.
- A non-finite or unreadable sample refuses rather than being skipped. Skipping one of three series produces a
  **partial sum**, which is the same failure as the fabricated zero.
- An arrival rate refuses a single sample, a zero interval, and a counter that went backwards — the last is a restart.

**`record` — the decision record section 9 requires**, refusing at write time rather than at claim time, because the
alternative is finding out when the claim is attempted and the data is already collected.

- `selection_probability` outside `(0, 1]` is refused. It cannot be reconstructed later: a deterministic policy's
  propensity is 1 for what it chose and the counterfactual arm has no data at all.
- `label_state` is `labelled` / `missing` / `pending`, and a label given with a state that does not admit one is
  refused. A missing label read as a failure moves a realised success rate in the direction that flatters the floor.
- The log is append-only, and a label that **changes** is refused rather than the later line winning. Without that the
  log is append-only in bytes and mutable in meaning, and the argument for trusting a criterion computed over it does
  not hold. A label arriving after `pending` is not a change.
- One corrupt line does not destroy the intact records around it. A crash mid-append leaves a truncated line, and the
  count travels rather than being swallowed.

**`accept` — section 12's criteria, computed, with a third verdict.**

`pass`, `fail`, and `unsupported`: the log does not contain what the criterion needs, so nothing was evaluated. A
checker with two verdicts must choose between reporting a pass it did not earn and a failure it cannot substantiate.

Three criteria are evaluable from a small log — no false certification (the falsifier), default-is-not-a-hiding-place,
and exploration cost. Six are not, and each says what it needs in its own words rather than sharing a message: a reader
told "insufficient data" learns nothing about what to collect. `summarise` deliberately does not reduce to `accepted`.

Floor compliance asks two questions that are not each other's negation: it **fails** on the exact one-sided binomial
tail and **passes** only when an exact lower confidence bound clears the floor. An earlier draft passed when the failure
test did not reject — accepting a null — and gated the middle case on `n < 30`, a constant nobody derived.

**`serve.route_once` — the composition.** Returns both the raw decision and the record: the first carries the gaps, the
second carries what a later claim is computed from. Both kinds of gap travel into the record, the policy's own and the
collector's, since a caller that saw one would think the other had been checked.

**CLI: `observe`, `assign`, `accept`.** And `decide.from_dict`, because a compiled policy on disk was not a policy
again: `as_dict` wrote guards only as prose, so the deployable path had to rebuild the object it had just serialised.

## Two defects found by wiring the parts together

Neither module read wrong on its own.

**The collector emitted bare state keys** while `decide`'s guards read them qualified by candidate (`inflight:box`). So
every request would have taken the default while the metrics endpoint answered perfectly — the failure mode that looks
like a working system. Caught by a test that calls both.

**`as_dict` was one-way.** Round-tripping is now a test, and an artifact written by the older shape refuses to load
rather than having its English parsed.

## Seven defects a review round found, all real

The review is in `docs/reviews/v0.1.0-review-fable.md`. The largest was invisible by construction.

**`candidate_set` fabricated its exclusion reasons.** It wrote `below_floor` for anything it could not otherwise
classify, **without a floor to compare against**, and four of the eight reasons were unreachable from that path — so the
log showed a clean, aggregable distribution over three reasons no matter what actually happened. A closed vocabulary of
invented values aggregates confidently into nonsense, and the membership check that guarantees the enum is exactly what
made the fabrication invisible. The reason now comes from the same `admissible` function the falsifier uses, and where
no floor is supplied it is `not_evaluated` — the true statement.

The rest: `bound_kind` was asserted as `lcb` for whatever a caller passed, so a log of point estimates claimed to be a
log of corrected lower bounds; `state_ref` was a dangling pointer to an observation nothing persisted; the
hiding-place scan skipped the chosen candidate, which made the purest case undetectable — an uncertified decision whose
own choice was admissible is the mechanism declining to certify something it could have — and it stopped at the first
violation; `admissible` ignored freshness although `evidence_expired` sat in the vocabulary with nothing able to produce
it; and the log and collector defects listed above.

And the over-claim: an earlier draft said **"the loop is closed"**, which is wrong and is corrected in the README and
CHANGELOG. Nothing feeds back. One turn runs end to end; a pipeline that cannot update anything from its own log is a
pipeline.

## Also in this release

`fix: an internal gateway hostname was baked into an example and a manifest`. Two occurrences were already public from
an earlier push and this work added a third. The example now uses a name that cannot resolve, and the manifest takes its
upstream from the environment and refuses to start without it — a literal there was both an environment-specific value
in a public manifest and a footgun.

## What is deliberately still missing

`decide.MISSING_FOR_A_CLOSED_LOOP` names six gaps and this closes the first. The other five stand: logged selection
probabilities that **vary**, exploration or shadow allocation, anytime-valid bounds, change-point detection, and a value
for the reserved candidate's scarce capacity. They are why six of nine acceptance criteria report `unsupported`, and the
report says so per criterion rather than in a footnote.

The propensity gap is the load-bearing one. Every decision a deterministic policy makes has propensity 1, under which
the counterfactual arm has no data and an off-policy estimate is unidentified. Randomised exploration is the
prerequisite for spend regret, not a refinement of it.

Two backlog items are written up in `docs/issues/`: reading the box's hidden state, to find out whether it breaks an
abstention limit that measured `AUC 0.5000` for a structural reason; and deriving the tie band from a jackknife over
already-recorded cohorts, so `--margin` stops being a value a human supplies.

## How to see it work

```console
tierbook observe --candidate box --metrics-url http://vllm:8000/metrics \
    --model-name <served-model> --authorised true --measured-on 2026-09-08 --out obs.json

tierbook assign --policy policy.json --request-id r1 --candidate box \
    --metrics-url http://vllm:8000/metrics --model-name <served-model> \
    --authorised true --previous obs.json --floor 0.80 --bound-kind lcb95 \
    --bounds '{"box":0.90,"api":0.70}' --costs '{"box":0.004,"api":0.012}' --log decisions.jsonl

tierbook accept --log decisions.jsonl --floor 0.80 --uncertified-tolerance 0.10
```

The first `observe` refuses the arrival rate and says why; the second, given `--previous`, produces one. Artifacts from
the verification run are in `docs/verify/`.

## Review notes for a reader

The three places most worth a second opinion:

1. **`accept.floor_compliance`'s two questions.** Failing on a tail and passing on a bound is deliberate, and it makes
   `pass` much harder to reach — 36 of 40 against a floor of 80% is `unsupported`, not a pass.
2. **`serve._why_not`'s ordering.** The cheapest facts first, so a candidate that is not serving is reported
   `unavailable` rather than as whatever its bound would have said. A different order would give a different, equally
   aggregable, and less useful log.
3. **Whether `unsupported` is used anywhere it should not be.** The review checked and found the errors ran the other
   way — passes too thin, never an unsupported that was actually computable — but it is the judgement most worth
   disagreeing with.
